# Nate Mills voice — distilled reference

Source: `ColdCallVids.md`, `comedyvideos.md`, `lessonvideos.md`, `500k+viewsvids.md`, `personalconversationvideos.md` (~90 short-form video breakdowns). This file is the condensed version for writing site copy — read the source files directly if you need a specific transcript or example.

## Who he is on camera

A sales rep talking to other sales reps, not a coach talking down to students. Everything is first-person and specific ("I just requested 5 days off," "that was me today"), never third-person advice-giver voice. He's currently doing the job, not reminiscing about having done it.

## The core structural pattern

Almost every piece of content — tactical, comedic, or emotional — follows the same shape:

**Absolute/contrarian statement → concrete example or story → tension/vulnerability → reframe → confident, often blunt, closing line.**

Examples of the opening statement alone: "Sales is the only job where you feel guilty about taking PTO." "Cold calling is simple." "If you work in sales, stop saying the F word." No throat-clearing, no "hey guys welcome back" — the first line is the hook and the thesis at once.

## Tone

- **Blunt, declarative, peer-level.** Short sentences. Contractions. Talks *to* "you," not about "salespeople" in the abstract.
- **Specific over vague, always.** He replaces principles with exact scripts ("say this instead") constantly — see Alternative Frameworks in `lessonvideos.md`. A rule of thumb repeated across the dataset: *specific scripts outperform general advice.*
- **Anti-corporate-buzzword.** Actively mocks vague manager-speak ("just add more value," "we're like a family here," "following up"). Never uses those phrases sincerely.
- **Self-aware about the absurdity of sales**, and that self-awareness *is* the confidence — he doesn't hide the awkwardness of cold calling, he names it and moves through it anyway ("There's no way this works... How are you?").
- **Emotionally honest about the bad parts** (PTO guilt, burnout, feeling behind, losing deals after months of work) without ever landing on cynicism — the reframe always circles back to "and I still love it" / "and you get back to it tomorrow." Resilience, not toxic positivity.
- **Profanity is casual and earned, not decorative** — "fucking simple," "this shit is simple." It shows up at moments of emphasis, not throughout.
- **Numbered lists as a default teaching device** — "Number one... number two... number three" — because his audience wants copy-pasteable material, not a lecture.
- **Closing lines land like a single punch**, not a summary: "God, I fucking love it." "That's just the reality of working in sales." "Guys, this shit is simple."

## Vocabulary

**Reach for:** pipeline, quota, cold call, discovery call, deal, commission, momentum, gatekeeper, objection, quota, PIP, "the reset," "back at zero."

**Avoid:** "synergy," "circle back," "following up," "add value," "leverage" (as a verb), any phrase he explicitly satirizes in the comedy set — those are jokes in his world, not copy you can use straight.

## What NOT to import wholesale

This is TikTok/Reels script voice, tuned for 15–60 second retention and shock/curiosity hooks. Site copy is read, not watched, and has to build trust with two different audiences (candidates vs. hiring companies) rather than stop a scroll. Translate the *energy* — directness, zero fluff, respect for the reader's time, occasional edge — don't transplant profanity or shock-opener tactics onto a form or a pitch page. The "good news or bad news" / "throw your phone out the window" style pattern-interrupts work because they're spoken and disposable; in permanent written copy the same move reads as gimmicky.

## How this should shape site copy

- **`candidates.html`** is the closer fit for his voice — its audience already follows him, already knows the pipeline/quota/cold-call vocabulary, and will read corporate-recruiter-speak as a red flag. Direct, peer-level, no "we are excited to announce" energy. Light edge is fine; heavy profanity is not (it's still a form people submit a resume through).
- **`employers.html`** talks to a different audience (hiring managers, not reps) — keep the directness and zero-fluff instinct, but drop the insider sales-culture references; a company exec won't clock "pipeline review" the way an SDR does.
- Prefer a concrete promise or scenario over an abstract value statement anywhere on the site — this is the single most consistent pattern across ~90 videos.
